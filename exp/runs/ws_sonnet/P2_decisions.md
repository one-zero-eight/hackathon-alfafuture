# P2 Decisions: Head of Procurement & Supply Chain, Volga pasta and bakery producer

Persona context: buys durum wheat, flour, packaging film and energy; exports to Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and China by rail and truck. Authority: suppliers, contracts and forward purchases, inventory buffers, logistics routes and carriers, export shipment planning.

## 1. Key issues

### K1. Domestic grain is turning up while southern export terminals are shut, and government support is arriving
- Items: A0127, A0366, A0756, A0865, A1556, A0503, A0935, A1909
- Transmission: Russian wheat prices rose 0.4-1.2% this week after three months of decline (A0127). Farmers who have already sold their minimum volume may refuse low-price deals, so slow growth is expected to continue.
- Azov-Black Sea grain terminals are effectively stopped. Transshipment fell 95-100% in September (A0865, A0756, A1556). Grain that cannot leave through the south stays in the domestic market, which restrains prices. The Black Sea closure also cuts Ukrainian supply, about 10% of world wheat (A0503), which supports world prices.
- Government support (A0366) includes up to 3 Mt of intervention-fund purchases in October, subsidised short-term loans for grain purchases, and per-hectare payments. These aim to stabilise domestic prices and put a floor under them. Super El Nino drought and flood risk is a longer-term upside risk for wheat (A0935, A1909).
- Why now: the three-month price decline has reversed, and the intervention purchases and the farmers' reluctance to sell cheaply both point to a floor. The window for cheap forward cover may be closing.

### K2. Kazakhstan and Central Asia export routes: border friction and a shift from road to rail, on a rail network that is stretched
- Items: A0826, A0123, A0126, A1408, A0584 (rel-2 SCO note), A0892, A1068, A1154, A0366, A1450 (weak signal)
- Transmission: Tyumen customs turned back 713 trucks from Kazakhstan for QR-code failures (A0826). The shipments in that item are inbound, but it shows the EAEU QR-code regime is being enforced strictly. Check whether the same regime applies to our outbound documents.
- Road freight demand rose 20% in Q3, and cargo is moving from road to rail, especially toward China and Central Asia. Border crossing with Kazakhstan is cited as a cost driver (A0123).
- Rail capacity is being redirected: loading plans for October are up on the Privolzhskaya, Kuibyshevskaya and Yuzhno-Uralskaya railways, and the Eastern polygon is at its limit (A0126). Our Volga origin sits on the affected network, so congestion and rail queues are likely.
- Rail transport subsidies are coming (A0366). The new transport minister Yadrov is pro-Eastern polygon expansion (A1068, A1154). Kazakhstan's QazETA entry authorisation affects driver and crew documents (A1408). Border-queue examples such as Verkhny Lars (A1450) show how quickly queues build.
- Why now: rail is becoming the default export mode just as capacity is cut. Booking decisions for Q4 and Q1 need to be made before the slots go.

### K3. Fuel and diesel: a bifurcated outlook with a mix of reassuring and worrying signals
- Items: A0004, A0790, A1842, A0346, A0574, A1271, A1119, A0896, A0906, A0914, A0964, A1132, A1547, A1760, A1697, A1469, A1519, A1734, A0453, A0649, A1072, A1364, A1922, A1807
- Reassuring: Ministry of Finance wants to scrap the import damper (A0346). FAS says exports will not raise domestic prices (A0574). Novak says only surplus will be exported (A1271, A1119). Putin says world prices fell 5-6% (A0896, A0906). Domestic diesel is -0.1% w/w at 86.58 rub/l (A1469). Gasoline limits are being lifted in Voronezh and Lipetsk (A1760, A1697).
- Worrying: refinery strikes resumed (A0004), though the US is demanding Ukraine stop (A0790, A1842). The US is releasing its emergency diesel reserve (A1364). The EU fuel price is +31.4% y/y (A1072). Brent is above $107-111 (A0453, A0649). Italian trucker strikes show what a fuel shock does to pasta supply chains (A1922, A1807). The government is considering limits on exchange trading of fuel (A1547).
- Transmission: carrier fuel surcharges, own plant energy, and packaging film (polymer) cost via oil.
- Why now: the diesel export ramp-up (300 kt in October, 500 kt in November, then 1 Mt) is the first real test of the "surplus only" claim. Our carrier contracts' fuel clauses and Q4 trucking rates depend on it.

### K4. Packaging film: oil up, ruble stronger, Chinese polymer capacity expanding
- Items: A0727, A0453, A0649, A0311, A1342, A1570, A1327, A0323, A0606
- Transmission: Brent at a 5-month high raises resin cost (A0453, A0649). Chinese polymer capacity growth is expected to lower prices over the medium term (A0727). The ruble is at its strongest in two months, with yuan around 12.2-12.35 and USD below 82 (A0311, A1342, A1570), which lowers imported film cost in rubles.
- Why now: near-term oil pushes film up while the stronger ruble offsets it. The medium-term capacity story argues against long fixed-price commitments. Shorter, index-linked terms are better.

### K5. Export demand and market-access signals: China, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan
- Items: A0823, A0455, A0584, A0316, A1228, A0892, A0165, A0441, A1801, A0037
- Transmission: Russia-China agri trade is up by more than a third over nine months, with the share of national currency near 100% (A0823). SCO trade is +18% with mostly national-currency settlement (A0455). Border modernisation with China is on the agenda (A0892). Dairy exports to Kazakhstan hit records (A0165), a sign of demand for Russian food there. Uzbekistan is moving to a new Latin alphabet (A1228), which can matter for pack labelling over time. The Uzbekistan-US deals include rail equipment localisation (A0316). The "hellish sanctions" law is frozen (A0037), which lowers secondary-tariff risk for China-bound trade for now.
- Why now: demand is positive. The constraint is logistics, not demand, so prioritise capacity.

### K6. Infrastructure and domestic delivery risk: drones, small-lot deliveries, alternative corridors
- Items: A0658, A0855, A0300, A0310, A1569, A1068, A0549, A0680, A1057, A1403
- Transmission: Krasnoe & Beloe cut accepted lot size to 25-30% of previous and wants more frequent deliveries (A0658, A0855). If other retail customers copy this, our logistics cost per tonne rises. Caspian port security concerns (A0300) and the Rasht-Astara and "Trump Route" corridors (A0310, A1569) are longer-term alternatives, not near-term. Hormuz and the Iran war keep energy prices high (A0549, A0680, A1057, A1403).
- Why now: the retail behaviour change is early and limited to one chain. It is a watch item, not an action item.

## 2. Decisions

1. **Layer in forward cover for durum and flour now (K1: A0127, A0366, A0756, A0865, A0935).** Raise cover from the current level toward roughly 50-60% of Q1-Q2 needs in tranches over 2-3 weeks. Prefer fixed price with a delivery schedule. Use the subsidised grain-purchase credit lines (A0366) if our banks are eligible. Do not go to 100% yet. Domestic prices are still below the three-month peak and the intervention fund and port closure cut both ways. This needs a combination of items: price turn (A0127), floor mechanics (A0366), and trapped supply (A0756, A0865).
2. **Diversify durum and flour suppliers geographically and by origin (K1).** Add at least one supplier outside the most weather-exposed region and secure optionality in contracts (volume +/- 15%). Reason: El Nino climate risk (A0935, A1909) and terminal closures (A0756) can cause local squeezes.
3. **Reserve rail capacity and shift export planning toward rail for Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and China, now through Q1 (K2: A0123, A0126, A0366, A1068).** Book monthly rail slots early and ask carriers about subsidy pass-through (A0366). Cap exposure to spot trucking. Keep a limited truck quota for urgent orders. Raise export lead times by roughly 1-2 weeks in the plan until rail congestion is clear.
4. **Audit export documentation and driver/crew compliance on the Kazakhstan road route within 1-2 weeks (K2: A0826, A1408).** Check QR-code and e-document practice with forwarders, confirm drivers' QazETA status with Kazakh counterparts, and add a pre-departure checklist. A turned-back truck means a lost week and spoiled schedule.
5. **Re-paper carrier and energy contracts with fuel indexation and caps (K3: A0346, A0574, A1271, A0004, A0453, A1547).** Move to a formula tied to published diesel prices with a monthly reset and a ceiling, rather than ad hoc surcharges. Do not prepay or lock in long fixed fuel rates, because the damper repeal, export ramp-up and refinery strikes make the direction uncertain. Review again after the first 300 kt export tranche is delivered.
6. **Keep packaging film purchases short and flexible (K4: A0727, A0453, A0311, A1342).** Buy 2-3 months of film at the current ruble level, because the ruble is strong and oil is rising. Avoid 12-month fixed deals. Request quotes from Chinese suppliers (capacity glut, A0727) and Russian converters, and index to resin price. This needs combining oil (A0453), FX (A0311, A1342) and capacity (A0727).
7. **Increase finished-goods and packaging safety stock for Central Asia shipments by about 1-2 weeks and revise delivery lot sizes with retail customers (K2, K6: A0126, A0826, A0658, A0855).** Hold a modest buffer at an export-oriented warehouse. Agree with Russian retail customers on delivery schedules before they impose smaller lots unilaterally.
8. **Prepare a response to the Italian pasta disruption (K3: A1922, A1807).** Ask the sales team whether Italian output losses create export or import-substitution opportunities in Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan or elsewhere, and whether durum supply from Italy-linked traders is tightening. This is a low-cost opportunity check, not a capacity commitment.

## 4. Traps

- **A0126 (coal to Baltic -80%)**: the headline is about coal, but the useful part is the rail-redistribution plan. Do not read it as a coal problem. Use it only as a congestion indicator for the Volga rail network.
- **A0826 (713 trucks turned back)**: the trucks were inbound to Russia from Kazakhstan, not our outbound exports. The QR-code point is a compliance signal, not proof that our shipments are at risk.
- **A1450 (133 trucks at Verkhny Lars)**: this is the Georgian border, not on our routes. Background only.
- **A0316 (Uzbekistan-US $12bn deals)**: headline number is large, but there is no sign of change in food import rules or rail tariffs. Do not alter Uzbekistan plans.
- **A1228 (Uzbek Latin alphabet)**: the note suggests relabelling, but the item gives no timeline or label requirement. Check later with local customers. Do not spend on packaging redesign now.
- **A0896/A0906 ("world prices fell 5-6%")**: a political claim by Putin, not a market data point. Do not lock in lower fuel assumptions on this basis.
- **A1271/A1119/A0574 (domestic diesel unaffected)**: official reassurances, contradicted by regional gasoline limits (A1760, A1697) and the refinery strikes. Keep fuel clauses protective.
- **A0165, A0441 (record dairy and Syria non-alcoholic beer exports)**: unrelated product categories. They show export appetite but not pasta demand.
- **A0939 (Acron and PhosAgro on fertilizer for the US)**: stock price move. Fertilizer export plans have only a distant link to our wheat cost.
- **A0286, A0327, A0340 (electricity tariff proposal, tax package, candy excise)**: none changes our costs now. The sugar VAT proposal was denied by the Ministry of Finance (A0340).
- **A1364, A1072, A0972 (US reserve, EU fuel prices, UK gas)**: background for global energy, with no direct transmission to our contracts.
- **A0310, A1569, A0300 (Rasht-Astara, Trump Route, Caspian ports)**: long-dated corridor projects. No effect on this quarter's routing.
- **A0813, A1003 (Armenia and the EAEU)**: not our markets.
- **A1524 (driverless trucks)**: long-term only.
- **A0822/A1409 (peace talks)**: hopeful headlines, but no concrete change to logistics or sanctions yet.
