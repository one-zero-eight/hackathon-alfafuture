# P6 Gold Memo: Head of HR and Corporate Security, French retail chain (140 stores, ~4,500 staff)

Week of 16-21 Oct 2026. Source: the full screened corpus (Russian-language coverage of French and European unrest).

## 1. Key issues

1. **Looting of shops on 17-18 Oct, nationwide** (A0225, A0367, A0050, A0137, A1192, A0990, A1597, A1562). Macron says shops were looted and retail outlets attacked. More than 2,000 shops across France were hit (A0367). Roughly half a million to 800,000 people marched (A0137, A0616). City halls were stormed in Lille and Marseille and burned in Limoges, and there was fighting on Place de la Republique in Paris (A0050, A0225, A0512). How it reaches us: we have stores in exactly these big cities, so the risk is direct damage, stolen stock, and staff caught up in violence. Why now: the 17 Oct pattern of daytime marches turning into looting is the template for the next round.

2. **"Act 2" national protest on Saturday 24 Oct, expected to be bigger** (A0605, A0593, A0616, A1935). Unions and left parties say they will bring out more than 1 million people, and yellow vests are joining the students (A0616). The PM promises a "massive" police deployment (A0605). Sentinelle soldiers will take over static site guarding so police can work the streets (A0593). The interior minister promises an "extremely tough" response, and a ban on unauthorised demonstrations is being considered (A1935). How it reaches us: Saturday is our peak trading day, and the same cities and stores are exposed again. This is the single most decision-relevant date.

3. **Curfews and assembly bans change opening hours and commutes** (A0251, A0475, A1272, A1504, A1873, A1976, A0510). Curfews are "local" and "partial" and were brought in specifically to protect shops (A0251). They have applied in central Paris, Lyon and Lille, and other cities are being added night by night, decided by prefects with mayors (A0475). The Paris curfew was extended a third night, 23:00-06:00 around Place de la Republique. Unauthorised gatherings are banned 20:00-06:00 near the Elysee, Champs-Elysees and railway stations (A1873). A student march on Thursday was banned, citing the terror threat (A1976). How it reaches us: late-closing stores and closing shifts in curfew zones, staff getting home, and legal compliance. The rules change daily, so we need a process, not a single decision.

4. **Possible state of emergency** (A0376, A0170, A0266). The PM does not rule it out. Macron kept his interior minister and ordered a change in crowd-control methods. How it reaches us: a state of emergency would bring wider restrictions on movement and gatherings, which affects store operations and staff travel. The legal trigger should be planned for now.

5. **Unrest is rooted in cost of living, so labour relations and pay are exposed** (A1598, A0336, A0453, A0649, A0661, A0529, A1337, A0957, A1240, A1939, A0235). The protests are against cost of living, fuel prices and pay (A0376, A1598). Brent is above $111 (A0453) and European gas is at a 2022 high (A0661). France is securing emergency diesel imports (A0529), a sign of fuel stress that hits commuting and store deliveries. French 10-year yields are at 5%, which points to austerity (A1337). Le Pen leads and calls the state out of control (A0957, A1579). How it reaches us: our mostly low-paid retail staff share these grievances. Expect pressure for transport or fuel allowances, union mobilisation, and walkouts by staff who join the protests. Several weak signals here add up to one strong one.

6. **Contagion and supply spillover across Europe** (A0030, A0085, A0139, A0252, A0389, A0551, A0578, A1369, A1580, A1807, A1357, A1721, A1758). School riots have spread to Belgium. Milan saw looted boutiques that lost more than EUR 700k and closed at 17:00 (A0252), and troops were sent in. Italian trucker strikes over diesel have emptied shelves and left airports short of fuel. There were riots in Spain. How it reaches us: a precedent for closing early, a risk to goods coming from or through Italy, and travel for staff in Italy, Belgium and Spain.

7. **Courts and police are at capacity, and looted goods are being resold** (A1620, A0367, A1339, A1223). Holding cells are full in Paris, Lille, Marseille, Bordeaux and Toulouse. Police have limited capacity to protect ordinary shops, while visible guarding goes to politically sensitive sites (A1339). Stolen goods are listed on Vinted, Leboncoin, Amazon and eBay, and the government wants the listings removed (A0367). How it reaches us: we have to rely on our own security, and documenting losses for police and insurers pays off.

## 2. Decisions

1. **Saturday 24 Oct posture** (A0605, A0593, A0616, A1935, combined with A0225, A0367). Close or reduce hours at high-exposure stores, meaning city centres on march routes in Paris (Republique, Champs-Elysees, near stations), Lyon, Lille, Marseille, Bordeaux, Toulouse and Limoges. Set an early close of about 15:00-16:00 at the other big-city stores (the Milan 17:00 precedent is in A0252). Decide by Thursday 22 Oct and confirm Friday after the prefecture announcements. Pull high-value stock from windows, lower shutters, and board up windows at the stores that were hit on 17 Oct.
2. **Daily curfew-compliance cell** (A0251, A0475, A1873, A1504). Starting now, someone checks prefecture and mayor curfew maps every afternoon. Closing times are set so staff are home before curfew starts, with at least 1-2 hours of margin. Stores are told by 16:00.
3. **Staff protection and commuting** (A0616, A0529, A1873). Use minimum crews on protest days, made up of volunteers. Don't schedule lone closing shifts. Pay taxis or travel home where the metro is closed. Staff may leave if they feel unsafe, with no loss of pay. Remind managers that staff must not confront looters and should keep themselves safe rather than defend stock.
4. **Crisis communication to employees** (A0225, A1935, A0376). Before Friday 23 Oct, send an SMS and app alert setting out the store-status list, how closures are announced, emergency contacts, and what to do if a store is attacked. Prepare an additional message for a possible state of emergency.
5. **Security reinforcement** (A1620, A1339, A0593). Book extra guards for Saturday and the following weekends at the roughly 20-30 most exposed stores. Don't expect a fast police response.
6. **Pay and labour relations** (A1598, A0453, A0661, A1337, A0336). Hold an early meeting with staff representatives on a temporary transport and fuel allowance, and compensate hours lost to closures. This takes the edge off cost-of-living grievances before unions mobilise. Agree a rule for staff who strike or protest. Don't take a wage-freeze stance (A0235 is a different sector and context).
7. **Loss documentation** (A0367). Log looted SKUs and file police complaints. Report stolen-goods listings on Vinted and Leboncoin through the government process.
8. **Travel advisory** (A0085, A0139, A1807, A0758). Postpone non-essential staff travel to Paris on protest days and to Milan and Rome. Warn travellers about fuel shortages at Italian airports and higher airfares.

## 3. Monitor
- Prefecture curfew and ban orders, nightly, for Paris and other cities (A1873, A0475).
- Whether a state of emergency is declared (A0376).
- The scale of the 24 Oct turnout, and whether more than 1 million attend (A0616, A0605).
- Local flashpoints, such as the Bobigny court protest over detained students (A1745) and student bans challenged in court (A1976).
- Fuel prices and supply: Brent, diesel imports, and any trucker action in France on the Italian model (A0453, A0529, A1580).
- Spread to Belgium and Spain (A0030, A1721), and union calls for strikes.

## 4. Traps
- Russian MFA and embassy warnings (A1017, A0587). These are advisories for Russian citizens, written to make a political point. They are not an independent read of security.
- Kremlin and propaganda framing, such as "France on fire", "post-apocalypse" and Kyiv-2014 comparisons (A0050 framing, A0263, A0551, A0246, A0803, A0336, A1240). The underlying facts are useful but the scale is exaggerated.
- The Macron family chocolate shop story (A0990, A1339, A1562) and the bunker piece (A0699). These are scandal angles. The only useful takeaway is how police resources are allocated.
- Russian tourist demand (A0385, A0614). Not relevant to our footfall or our staff.
- Arguments over foreign involvement and Trump's comments (A0512, A0688, A1598, A1701, A0807, A1758). Political noise.
- The Volkswagen wage freeze (A0235). It concerns another sector and the German auto industry, and is not a benchmark for French retail pay.
- Bond yields and Bessent (A0612, A1337). This is macro background only and should not drive any action this week.
