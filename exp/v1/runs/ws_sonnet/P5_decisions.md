# P5 (COO, Almaty freight forwarder, China–Kazakhstan–Russia and Middle Corridor) – whole-week decision memo

## 1. Key issues

**1. Southern Russian ports are paralysed, and cargo is shifting to land corridors (capacity and pricing opportunity, but also congestion).**
- Items: A0865, A1556, A0756, A0126, A0034, A0239, A0900, A0503, A0644, A1391, A0408, A1485.
- Transmission: Azov-Black Sea terminal throughput fell 95-100% in September, with losses above 200 bn RUB a month. Cargo is moving to the North-West and to land crossings. Black Sea attacks (tankers, Sochi, NATO concern, strikes on ships going to Odesa) will not stop soon.
- Rail loading is being redirected to Baltic ports, and coal to the Baltics may be cut 80% (A0126). The Eastern polygon is "at the limit".
- A0126 also reports a planned suspension of priority coal transit from Kazakhstan. This is a direct hit to Kazakh-origin rail flows and needs checking.
- Why now: Q3 trucking demand rose 20% (A0123). Freight is moving from road to rail, especially toward China and Central Asia. We can price capacity at a premium, but rail slots on the Russian network will be scarce.

**2. The China–Kazakhstan–Russia border and compliance friction is rising on our core legs.**
- Items: A0123, A0826, A1408, A0892, A0584, A0455, A0800, A1592.
- A0123: delivery from China is more expensive because of problems crossing the Kazakhstan border.
- A0826: Tyumen customs turned back 713 Kazakh trucks over QR-code presentation rules (EAEU road imports), out of about 40k trucks since June (~1.8%).
- A1408: Kazakhstan's QazETA e-permit rules apply to foreigners from 85 visa-free states, including Russia. Driver and crew compliance is affected.
- Policy side: Russia and China want to modernise border crossings and digitise rail (A0892, A0584). Payments and customs friction is acknowledged at the SCO (A0584). RUB/CNY settlement is close to 100% (A0800, A1592).
- Why now: the QR-code rule is a documentation-process risk we can fix cheaply, and QazETA is a pending crew risk.

**3. Fuel costs: high crude, a Russian diesel export deal, and contradictory domestic signals.**
- Items: A0453, A0649, A1925, A0354, A0004, A0031, A0122, A0790, A1842, A0346, A0574, A0896, A0863, A0914, A0964, A1271, A1119, A1547, A1072, A1364, A0578, A1469, A1697, A1760, A0729.
- Brent hit 111 USD (5-month high), then fell to about 108 (A0453, A0649), driven by Houthi/Aramco strikes and Hormuz mines (A0680).
- Ukrainian refinery strikes resumed (Ryazan A0004, Surgut A0031). The US is demanding they stop (A0790, A1842).
- Russia will export surplus diesel only: first 40k t tanker to the US on 22 Oct, 300k t in October, 500k t in November, then 1 mn t. Exports to other countries may be fully restored by year-end (A0964). Officials say the domestic market is unaffected (A0574, A1119, A1271), and Rosstat shows diesel -0.1% w/w to 86.58 RUB/l (A1469).
- Counter-signals: regional fuel limits are being lifted (A1697, A1760). The government is working on exchange trading limits and a diesel price-swing cap (A1547). The Minfin hopes to cancel the import damper (A0346).
- Why it matters: fuel costs on the Russian leg are volatile in both directions. The risk is a price spike if refinery strikes continue, versus a decline if the US deal and Russian truce hold (A0896 notes a 5-6% fall in world diesel prices). EU fuel is +31.4% y/y and Italian trucker protests (A0578, A1580, A1369, A1807) show European haulage disruption that affects our European clients.

**4. The sanctions environment is easing in the US, but it is fragile, and secondary-sanction and cargo screening risk is not gone.**
- Items: A0037, A0083, A0115, A0576, A1349, A1999, A0518, A0640, A0448, A0481, A0765, A0290, A1356, A1564, A1762, A1728, A0496, A1742, A1896, A0777, A0152, A0390, A1374, A1848, A0673, A0883, A0789, A1409.
- Trump suspended the Graham "hellish sanctions" law (waiver, with a possible return after 60 days: A0083). He will not tighten sanctions during talks (A1999). Trilateral Ukraine talks may resume in early November (A1848). A US-Russia-China summit may take place at APEC in Shenzhen on 18-19 Nov (A0152, A0390, A1374).
- Counter-signals: the EU keeps all sanctions and prepares a 22nd package targeting the shadow fleet, energy and banks (A0765, A0290). OFAC is still designating Iran-linked shipping and logistics firms (A1762, A1728), including a Dubai logistics line and a Singapore shipping agent. The OFAC Lukoil licence runs to 21 Nov (A1564). The US is lifting some Belarus listings (A1742, A1896).
- Transmission: our clients include European and Russian importers. A forwarder is exposed to counterparty and cargo screening. The relaxed US posture lowers the immediate secondary-sanctions risk on China-Russia flows, but the EU channel and OFAC Iran/shipping lists are tightening. The US stance can reverse (the law was only waived).

**5. Export-control and strategic-cargo screening: China's new drone controls take effect 18 Nov.**
- Items: A0285, A0474, A0967, A1728.
- China requires permits for UAVs, flight-control technology, engines and satellite navigation, and exporters must name end users (A0285). Rare-earth export restrictions persist (A0474, yttrium). Chip-related flows are licensed (A0967).
- Why it matters: China-origin shipments of such goods through Kazakhstan, and re-export to Russia, create dual-use liability for the forwarder. A screening rule has to be ready before 18 Nov.

**6. Caspian / Middle Corridor and southern alternatives: attractive in theory, with rising security and political risk.**
- Items: A0300, A0310, A1569, A0989, A0854, A1450, A0248, A0239, A0680, A1057, A1906, A1949, A1403, A0318), A0549.
- Iran's first VP asked Russia to protect Caspian ports from Ukrainian attacks (A0300), so the Caspian is now a perceived risk zone. Rasht-Astara rail land purchase is claimed done but documents are unconfirmed (A0310). The "Trump Route" (Armenia) has banned cargo categories and construction may start only in early 2027 (A1569). Verkhny Lars queue is 133 trucks (A1450). Russia-Georgia ties are tense (A0989), and Russian bans on Georgian drinks are in place (A0283).
- Iran/Gulf: Hormuz is mined and tankers are hit (A0680). The US plans embassy evacuations in the Gulf and explosions were reported at Sirik (A1906, A1949). Iran says Hormuz cannot be replaced (A1057), which supports land-route demand (A0318 Houthi/Red Sea).
- Why now: the Middle Corridor can capture Black Sea/Hormuz-displaced demand, but Caspian port security and Iran-related sanctions screening both affect it.

**7. Competing corridors and Russian transport policy.**
- Items: A0408, A0530, A0414, A0582, A1068, A0178, A1154, A0316.
- Northern Sea Route (NSR) oil volumes are rising (A0408). The EU wants access to the NSR (A0530, A0414). The new Transport Minister Yadrov (confirmed by the Duma, A1154) promises further Eastern polygon expansion and the Trans-Arctic corridor (A1068). Uzbekistan-US rail deals (Wabtec localisation) change regional capacity (A0316).
- Why it matters: the Eastern polygon is capped, so China-Russia rail capacity stays the binding constraint. This is background rather than a near-term decision driver.

**8. Demand and client-side signals.**
- Items: A0205, A0823, A1107, A0455, A0133, A0311, A1570, A0606, A0855, A0658, A0043, A0260.
- China exports are +36% in September, with a trade surplus record (A0205). Russia-China agri trade is up a third (A0823, A1107). China exports to Russia are +33% Jan-Sep (A0800). EU exports to Russia are -15.9% (A0133).
- The ruble is strengthening against the yuan (12.2-12.35: A0311, A1570), which supports Russian importers' ability to pay.
- Retailers are moving to smaller, more frequent deliveries because of drone risk (A0855, A0658). Airports are closed during drone raids (A0043), so air is not a reliable fallback. A St. Petersburg raid hit an oil terminal and an Ozon warehouse (A0260).

## 2. Decisions

1. **Reprice and reserve capacity on rail/truck China-Kazakhstan-Russia and Central Asia legs.** (A0865, A1556, A0126, A0123, A0756)
   - Introduce a southern-port-diversion surcharge or dynamic pricing now.
   - Pre-book rail slots and wagon capacity for Q4 (Nov-Dec) with Kazakh and Russian operators before diversion volumes fill them.
   - Size: target 15-25% of Q4 rail volume pre-booked; review the exact figure with commercial. This combines the port paralysis, the rail redirect to the Baltic, and the 20% Q3 trucking demand rise.

2. **Tighten QR-code and customs documentation on trucks entering Russia from Kazakhstan.** (A0826, A0123)
   - Run a pre-departure QR-code check for every Russia-bound truck.
   - Brief carriers this week; audit refusal causes. Customs data show a turn-back rate of about 1.8% of trucks, but each case costs a return trip.
   - Pass the cost into pricing where refusals are client-caused.

3. **Fix the crew and driver entry process for QazETA.** (A1408)
   - Check which of our drivers and foreign crews fall under QazETA. Ask the Kazakh MFA/immigration for the exact rules and timing. Prepare the permits in advance. Do this before the rules start to bite.

4. **Make fuel contracts more flexible and index fuel surcharges.** (A0453, A0649, A0004, A0896, A1271, A1547, A0346)
   - Use a fuel-indexed surcharge tied to the Russian diesel retail price or Brent rather than fixed fuel pricing, with a monthly reset.
   - Do not lock long fixed-price carrier contracts right now. The signals cut both ways (Brent 108-111 and refinery strikes up; US deal and ramping exports down).
   - Request price-cap clauses from carriers for the Russian leg. This needs several items combined: crude, refinery strikes, the export deal and regulatory interventions.

5. **Adopt a compliance screening upgrade (counterparties, cargo, shipping agents).** (A1762, A1728, A0765, A0290, A0037, A0083, A1564, A0285, A0474)
   - Refresh screening against the latest OFAC Iran-related designations (shipping/logistics firms, including a Dubai logistics line and Singapore agent).
   - Add EU 22nd-package watch for shadow fleet, energy and banks.
   - Add a China drone and dual-use export-control checklist with end-user declaration for shipments from 18 Nov.
   - Keep Russia-bound screening at full strength despite the US pause. The Graham-law waiver can be reversed after 60 days (A0083).
   - Requires combining US, EU and Chinese regimes.

6. **Prepare a Caspian/Middle Corridor contingency plan, but do not shift volumes yet.** (A0300, A0680, A1057, A1906, A1949, A0310, A1569, A1450)
   - Pre-qualify alternate Caspian ports and carriers. Add a security and war-risk clause for Caspian legs.
   - Set trigger criteria (a strike on a Caspian port, or an Iran-US escalation) to move volumes.
   - Offer Middle Corridor to European clients as an option for Black Sea and Hormuz diversions, with an explicit risk premium.
   - Verkhny Lars (133 trucks) is not a good fallback now.

7. **Customer communication and a payment-terms review for Russian importers.** (A0800, A1592, A0311, A1570, A0455, A0133)
   - RUB/CNY settlement is near-total, and the ruble is stronger. Keep invoicing in a currency that matches the client's cash flow. Offer RUB/CNY-denominated rates to Russian clients. Do not change terms for European clients (EU exports to Russia are down).

8. **Plan for the Nov-Dec calendar events and stage-gate larger investments.** (A1848, A0152, A1374, A1068, A1154)
   - Hold major capex or long-term capacity commitments until early-November talks and the APEC summit (18-19 Nov) clarify sanctions direction.
   - Keep scenario plans for both a sanctions easing (volume upside for Russia-bound flows) and a reversal.

## 4. Traps

- Medvedev's remarks on the Graham law and Nietzsche (A0448, A0481): rhetoric, no operational change.
- Ryabkov, Zakharova, Dmitriev statements (A0576, A0765, A1349): positioning, not policy.
- Gasoline price falls and regional limit lifting (A1469, A1519, A1697, A1760): gasoline, not diesel; diesel is the relevant fuel and it moved only -0.1%.
- Novak/FAS statements that the domestic diesel market will not be affected (A0574, A1119, A1271): official reassurance. Treat with caution while refineries are being hit and exports ramp up.
- Trump's pause on the Graham law (A0037, A0115, A1999): not a repeal; it can return after 60 days. Do not loosen screening on that basis.
- SCO and Russia-China trade-growth boilerplate (A0584, A0455, A0823, A0934, A1107, A1496): positive but non-specific; no committed volumes for us.
- Belaruskali delisting (A1742, A1896): potash-specific, little relevance to our lanes unless we carry potash.
- Armenia/Georgia political news (A0832, A0854, A1141): background; act only if EAEU rules change.
- Italy protests and shortages (A0578, A1580, A1369, A1807): only a modest effect on European clients' final delivery.
- NSR and EU Arctic strategy (A0408, A0530, A0414, A0582): long-horizon and not competitive with rail for our clients this season.
- Items about Russian Far East/other topics (A0232 Kyrgyz coup remark, A0727 plastics, A0535 ING/Gazprom, A1386 ICC award, A1524 robots, A0658): marginal.
- Border news from Estonia (A0958, A1559): not on our routes.
