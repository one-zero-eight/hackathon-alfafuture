# P6 decision memo: Head of HR and Corporate Security, French retail chain (140 stores, ~4,500 staff)

Week covered: 17-21 Oct 2026. Sources are Russian-language outlets, so items are secondhand reports of French events.

## 1. Key issues

**1. Looting wave of 17-18 Oct hit retail directly, and the next wave is scheduled for Sat 24 Oct ("Act 2").**
- Items: A0137, A0050, A0225, A0367, A0616, A1935, A0605, A0593, A1976.
- Scale reported:
  - 500-800k protesters on 17 Oct (A0137, A0225, A0616, A0605).
  - More than 2,000 shops hit (A0367).
  - City halls stormed in Lille and Marseille (A0050).
  - The Limoges town hall was set on fire (A0225).
- Unions and left parties promise more than 1 million people for 24 Oct. Gilets jaunes are expected to join (A0616, A1935).
- Transmission path:
  - Stores in the Paris region and large cities are the exact targets: looting, broken windows and arson.
  - The 24 Oct date falls on a Saturday, the peak trading and staffing day.
- Why now: it is Thursday 22 Oct, so there are about 48 hours to decide closures, shift plans and security.

**2. State response has changed the operating environment: curfews, bans and a military role.**
- Items: A0251, A0475, A0510, A1272, A1504, A1873, A1976, A0605, A0593, A0376, A0170.
- Paris curfews:
  - Local curfews were set up at Place de la République and Les Halles.
  - They were extended for a third night, 23:00-06:00 (A1873, A1272, A1504).
  - The prefecture also banned unauthorised gatherings from 20:00 on Wed 21 Oct to 06:00 on Thu 22 Oct. The ban covers areas around the Élysée, the Assemblée, the Sénat, the Champs-Élysées and train stations (A1873).
- Elsewhere:
  - Curfews or youth-movement restrictions cover Lyon, Lille, Marseille, Bordeaux and Nice (A0475, A0510).
  - More are to be decided locally by prefects and mayors each evening (A0475).
- Policing for 24 Oct:
  - Prefects are told to ban protests that could disturb order (A0605).
  - Sentinelle soldiers will guard fixed sites to free police (A0593).
  - A state of emergency has not been ruled out (A0376).
  - A student march on Thu 22 Oct was banned from 08:00 to 20:00, citing terror risk and riot risk (A1976).
- Transmission path:
  - Store hours, staff commutes and closing times are now set by prefectural orders that are renewed nightly.
  - The Les Halles shopping district is named, which is a likely location for the company's stores.
  - Home commutes after 23:00 and the closing shift are the exposed points.
- Operational note: Paris metro closures and burned cars were reported (A0616). Louvre closed (A0385).

**3. Looting-loss and stolen-goods follow-up: the loss pattern is coordinated and organised.**
- Items: A0367, A0252, A0389, A1583, A1516, A0455-type pattern from A0389.
- Details:
  - The government told platforms to remove listings of looted goods (Vinted, Leboncoin, Amazon, eBay) and met the Interior and Justice ministries (A0367).
  - Milan shows the pattern for clothing and accessories retail. Masked groups know brands. Boutiques were closed at 17:00. Losses were over EUR 700k and included lost sales from early closure (A0252, A0389).
  - Italian police are tracing looters through viral videos and ads (A1583).
- Transmission path:
  - Clothing and home-goods stores are the targeted category.
  - Early closure is itself a loss, so there is a cost to weigh against staff safety.
  - Evidence and resale tracking are a way to recover value.

**4. Duration and spread: this is a multi-week movement with a wide base, not a weekend event.**
- Items: A0030, A0050, A0616, A0587, A0614, A1504, A1843, A0957, A1240, A0170, A0266.
- Evidence:
  - Unrest began mid-September with lycéens in the Paris suburbs (A1504) and has widened to unions, gilets jaunes, drivers, medics, firefighters, civil servants and pensioners (A1017, A0246).
  - The embassy flags another date, 5 Nov, besides 24 Oct (A0614).
  - The interior minister offered his resignation. Macron refused it and ordered a change of crowd-control methods (A0170, A0266).
  - Le Pen leads polls by 18-22 points (A0957, A1240), so the political temperature stays high toward the 2027 election.
  - Newsweek ranks France first for protest potential (A1843).
  - Ongoing local flashpoints: a 200-person protest at Bobigny court over detained students (A1745). Cells are full in five cities (A1620).
- Transmission path: plan for rolling disruption through at least 5 Nov and probably beyond, so build repeatable procedures instead of one-off reactions.

**5. Root cause is cost of living, which feeds directly into labour relations and pay policy.**
- Items: A0225, A0251, A1598, A0453, A0649, A0661, A1337, A0235, A0336, A0050, A0137.
- Evidence:
  - Protests are against the cost of living, fuel prices and weak public services (A0251, A1598).
  - Brent is above $111 (A0453, A0649). EU gas is at its highest since 2022 (A0661).
  - The 10-year French yield reached 5%, the highest since 2002, with the 2027 budget not yet passed (A1337, A0612). That signals possible austerity and more social tension.
  - Volkswagen is freezing pay to 2030 (A0235). This is a benchmark that a French employer should not copy casually.
- Transmission path:
  - Retail staff are on modest pay and exposed to the same cost pressures.
  - Unions and staff are likely to push for pay, transport or fuel allowances.
  - Strikes that involved drivers, firefighters and others (A1017, A0246) can also hit commuting and deliveries.
- Why now: the mood is volatile, and employer handling of pay and safety this week will shape trust.

**6. Fuel and supply chain: diesel is tight in France, and Italy shows what a trucker strike does.**
- Items: A0529, A0578, A1580, A1807, A1369, A0758.
- Evidence:
  - France secured 295kt per month of diesel (about 10% of consumption, roughly a third of monthly imports) from Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Bahrain, for October-November (A0529). The stated purpose is to cut pump prices and reduce shortage risk.
  - In Italy, trucker and farmer protests over diesel emptied shelves in Rome, Milan and Naples and cut jet fuel at seven airports (A0578, A1580, A1807). Large chains held out thanks to warehouse stock (A0578).
  - Airfares in Europe may rise 10-20% (A0758).
- Transmission path:
  - A French trucker blockade would hit store deliveries and staff commuting.
  - Business travel for staff costs more.
- Note: this is a risk scenario, not a French event this week. The relevant owner for deliveries is supply chain, but HR is affected through commuting and scheduling.

**7. Duty of care for travel and staff abroad, plus information noise.**
- Items: A1017, A0587, A0614, A0385, A0246, A0807, A1701, A1598, A1712, A1758.
- Evidence:
  - The Russian foreign ministry and embassy tell people to avoid crowds in Europe and flag 24 Oct and 5 Nov (A1017, A0587, A0614). These are third-party advisories from a country with its own agenda. Use them only as a date confirmation.
  - Macron says there is no visible foreign interference, but there was "foreign manipulation" (A1712). Italy is probing a Russian link (A1758). Russian outlets blame Macron or the West (A1701, A0803).
- Transmission path: communication to staff must be factual and calm, and it should not feed rumours of foreign or ethnic blame. Trump and others blaming migrants (A0688, A1712) raise the risk of tension among staff.

## 2. Decisions

**D1. Set a store-by-store closure and early-release plan for Sat 24 Oct. Decide by Thu 22 Oct evening, confirm Fri 23 Oct.**
- Tied to: A1935, A0616, A0605, A0593, A0252, A0251, A0475.
- Action: classify the 140 stores by exposure.
  - Red: stores near Place de la République, Les Halles, prefectures, city halls, and the main shopping streets in Paris, Lyon, Lille, Marseille, Bordeaux, Nice and Toulouse. Close at midday or stay closed on 24 Oct.
  - Amber: stores in big-city centres. Close early, with a staff exit by about 16:00-17:00, which is the time Milan stores closed (A0252).
  - Green: other stores trade normally with enhanced watch.
- Trigger rules: any prefectural curfew or protest ban touching a store's area closes it for that period. Use daily prefecture and city announcements, because curfews are decided locally each evening (A0475).
- Combines several items: exposure map (A0050, A0225), date and size (A1935, A0616), and the Milan precedent for timing (A0252).

**D2. Protect staff on the way home and during shifts.**
- Tied to: A1873, A1272, A1504, A0616, A0475.
- Actions:
  - Stop closing shifts that end after about 21:00 in curfew zones. Staff must be home before 23:00 curfew hours or covered by the exemption letter for workers.
  - Provide the work-attestation document for staff who must travel during curfew. (A1272 states there are exceptions for residents and workers.)
  - Fund taxi or ride transport for late shifts in affected areas.
  - Allow remote work for head-office staff on 24 Oct.
  - Do not require staff in red stores to work. Pay them for cancelled shifts.
- Rationale: metro closures and burned cars (A0616) make normal commutes unreliable.

**D3. Strengthen physical security at amber and red stores for 23-25 Oct and again before 5 Nov.**
- Tied to: A0367, A0252, A0389, A0593, A0605, A0511-type guards from A1339.
- Actions:
  - Contract extra guards where available. Expect high demand, so order now.
  - Fit metal shutters or board up windows. Remove high-value stock from windows and front displays. Move stock to back rooms or other sites.
  - Check CCTV and alarm links. Clarify who has authority to order evacuation.
  - Ask the local prefecture or police for contact numbers. Police resources are stretched, and the army only guards fixed official sites (A0593). Do not assume police will protect stores. Note that police presence near a store is decided by local prefects, as the Amiens case shows (A1339).
- Combines several items: looting pattern (A0252, A0389), limited police (A0593, A0605).

**D4. Set up a looting-response and evidence protocol.**
- Tied to: A0367, A1583.
- Actions:
  - Keep CCTV footage and inventories ready for police and insurers.
  - Log stolen goods with identifiers so they can be matched to online listings.
  - Assign someone to report listings on Vinted, Leboncoin, Amazon and eBay, since the government is pressing platforms (A0367).
  - Notify the insurer now and confirm that riot and looting cover applies, and whether closure orders are covered. (Insurance terms are not in the items. Check them.)

**D5. Issue a staff communication now and then on a fixed rhythm.**
- Tied to: A0616, A1873, A1976, A0614, A1712, A0803.
- Content:
  - Which stores are open or closed on 24 Oct.
  - The right to refuse unsafe work and not be penalised.
  - Where to find curfew information.
  - A hotline and a named contact.
- Rhythm: daily at 17:00 for the next 3 days, then before each flagged date (24 Oct, 5 Nov).
- Tone: neutral. Do not comment on blame, migrants, or foreign interference (A1712, A0688, A1758).
- Also set up psychological support for staff who were present in looted stores.

**D6. Prepare a labour-relations stance for the cost-of-living demands before unions raise them.**
- Tied to: A0251, A1598, A0453, A0661, A1337, A0235, A0050.
- Actions:
  - Consider a one-off cost-of-living or transport bonus, or a fuel allowance for staff in high-cost areas. Size it with Finance. This is a proposal; the items give no pay data for this company.
  - Do not announce pay freezes in the current climate (A0235 is a benchmark, but copying it would invite conflict).
  - Brief store managers and union reps so that strike notices around 24 Oct and 5 Nov do not come as a surprise. Check the legal notice rules for strikes with counsel.
- Combines several items: cause (A0251, A1598), cost pressure (A0453, A0661), and fiscal outlook (A1337).

**D7. Set a standing crisis cell and a 5 Nov plan.**
- Tied to: A0605, A0614, A1843, A0170, A0030.
- Actions:
  - A named crisis team (HR, security, operations, communications, legal) meets daily until 6 Nov.
  - Reuse the 24 Oct plan for 5 Nov and any later dates.
  - Each evening, review prefectural decisions and set next-day store status by 18:00.
  - Review again after 24 Oct. If a state of emergency is declared (A0376), move to a stricter posture.

**D8. Adopt a travel rule for staff for the next two weeks.**
- Tied to: A1017, A0587, A0614, A0385, A0758.
- Actions:
  - Postpone non-essential staff travel into and between affected cities on protest dates.
  - Avoid crowds and route around the main squares. Route changes should favour the areas the tour operators describe as calm (A0385).
  - Budget for higher airfares (A0758) if travel continues.
- Note: the sources for the advisories are Russian bodies, so also check French government advice.

## 4. Traps

- Pro-Kremlin and tabloid commentary (A0263 Maidan comparison, A0336, A0803, A0551, A1701, A0699 bunker, A0990/A1339 Brigitte Macron shop story, A1223, A1562, A1357). These add no operational facts. The Amiens police-van story (A0990, A1339) is a political scandal; the only usable point is that police posture is decided locally.
- Russian tourist and tour-operator reports (A0385, A0614). They show Russian tourists keep travelling. That says nothing about French footfall or safety for the company, and tour operators have an interest in downplaying risk.
- Russian MFA and embassy warnings (A1017, A0587) as authoritative advice. They are useful only for the dates. Use French official sources for decisions.
- Sabotage and hybrid-war items (A0807, A1758, A1701) and the Albania story (A0512). Attribution is disputed. Macron says there is no visible foreign interference (A1712). They should not change store or staff measures.
- Volkswagen wage freeze (A0235). It is a different sector and country, and not a model for a French retailer under strike pressure.
- Trump and migrant-blame statements (A0688). They matter only for communication tone.
- Newsweek ranking (A1843). It is based mostly on 2024-25 data, from before the 2026 crises, so it is weak evidence.
- Italian shelf shortages (A0578, A1369, A1580, A1807) as if they were a French problem. They are a precedent only. The large Italian chains coped from stock (A0578).
- Airfare forecast (A0758). A single airline CEO's forecast with minor effect on this role.
- Bessent comment (A0612) and the Le Pen analysis items (A1579, A1240) as drivers of decisions. Context only.
