# Procurement memo, week of 18-21 Oct 2026

## 1. Decisions now

1. **Forward-buy durum/flour: 6-8 weeks cover, in tranches.** Buy 50% now at fixed price and the rest on any further weekly rise. Owner: grain buyer, by 31 Oct. Drivers: A0127, A0618. Path: domestic wheat ended a 3-month fall (+0.4-1.2% w/w) and farmers are refusing low bids, so the cheap window is closing. Check (rule 1 vs 2): the trend is turning up, so buy early, not wait. The rise is slow and the ruble is strong, so I stay short of the 2-4 month maximum and keep the rest on spot.
2. **Lock packaging film for 4-5 months, with an index cap.** Place a 60% volume contract and qualify a second supplier. Owner: packaging buyer, within 2 weeks. Drivers: A1072, A0453, A0661, A0727. Path: Brent above $111 (A0453), EU fuel prices up 31.4% y/y (A1072) and TTF above EUR 90 (A0661) point to polymer and energy cost pressure. Check (rule 4, cross-pattern c): the direction is up, so locking early is right. Review surcharge and indexation clauses before signing.
3. **Buy imported packaging and components now while the ruble is strong.** Pull forward Q4-Q1 orders, using the USD 82-83 rate. Owner: procurement with treasury, this week. Drivers: A0618, A0311, A1342. Path: the ruble is at a two-month high, so imports are cheaper. Check (rule 7): a strong ruble favors buying imports early. Exports lose margin, so I do not assume the rate holds (see Monitor).
4. **Renegotiate freight and add fuel surcharge clauses.** Consolidate loads and fix the diesel-linked share at a capped formula. Owner: logistics lead, within 3 weeks. Drivers: A0123, A1547, A1072. Path: diesel is volatile, domestic trucking demand is up 20%, and the government is studying diesel exchange limits. Check (rule 8): costs are rising, so clauses protect us. The cap should be symmetric if diesel falls.
5. **Book Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan rail slots 3 weeks ahead and pre-ship.** Move 2-3 weeks of export volume forward and keep trucks for urgent small lots. Owner: export logistics, this week. Drivers: A0123, A0455. Path: the Black Sea is closed and transit via Iran costs twice as much, so more cargo shifts to land routes. China-Kazakhstan border crossing is slow, so I expect wagon pressure. Check (rule 5): rail takes 1-3 weeks, so I must book now. Cross-pattern (b) applies if peak season coincides.
6. **Route China exports away from the Kazakhstan border where possible.** Ask carriers for alternative crossings and add 5-7 days to China lead times. Owner: export planning, within 2 weeks. Drivers: A0123. Path: crossing difficulty at the Kazakhstan border raises cost and delay on China shipments. Check (rule 5): the disruption is described as present, not forecast.
7. **Set safety stock at 4 weeks for imported film and key inputs.** Owner: planning, by 31 Oct. Drivers: A0123, A1485, A0453. Path: shipping and fuel risks raise supply uncertainty. Check (rule 11): measured in weeks. Storage cost is modest next to the price risk.
8. **Trim promotions that need heavy packaging usage until contracts (decision 2) are signed.** Owner: commercial director, 2 weeks. Drivers: A1072, A0661, A0453. Path: energy spike plus packaging cost pressure squeezes margin. Check (cross-pattern c): the direction is cost up, so protecting margin is correct. This is reversible.
