# P2 Gold Memo: Head of Procurement & Supply Chain, Volga pasta/bakery producer

## 1. Key issues

1. **Domestic wheat has bottomed and is turning up.** Items: A0127, A0366, A0756, A1556, A0865, A0503, A1545.
   - What happened: domestic wheat rose 0.4-1.2% w/w after three months of decline, and farmers are refusing low bids (A0127). The state will buy up to 3 Mt into the intervention fund in October and is considering subsidised credit for grain purchases (A0366).
   - Countervailing signal: Azov-Black Sea grain terminals are effectively halted, with transshipment down 95-100% in September (A0756, A1556, A0865). That keeps export grain inside Russia, which pushes prices down.
   - Path to the company: the state is pulling grain off the market while farmers hold back, against a glut caused by the export blockage. The net is a slow price rise. Retail pasta prices rose 0.4% in the week (A1545), so there is some room to pass costs through.
   - Why now: this is the window to lock in durum and flour forwards before the rise builds.

2. **The Kazakhstan border and road export channel is getting harder.** Items: A0826, A1408, A0123, A0584.
   - What happened: Tyumen customs turned back 713 trucks for QR-code non-compliance on EAEU road consignments (A0826). Kazakhstan's new QazETA electronic entry authorisation affects drivers (A1408). Crossing difficulties are raising China/Kazakhstan road costs (A0123). SCO customs frictions add to this (A0584).
   - Path to the company: rejected or late trucks, extra demurrage, driver eligibility problems.

3. **Freight is shifting from road to rail, and rail capacity is tight.** Items: A0123, A0126, A0366, A1068, A0892.
   - What happened: road freight demand rose 20% in Q3 with sharp price swings, and cargo is moving to rail, especially toward China and Central Asia (A0123). October loading from the Volga/Kuibyshev railways to the Baltic is planned up 39-42% (A0126), which competes for wagons.
   - Offsetting factors: a new tranche of rail-transport subsidies (A0366) and planned expansion of the Eastern polygon and China crossings (A1068, A0892).
   - Path to the company: wagon availability and rates for export shipments.

4. **Diesel and fuel prices are volatile with mixed signals.** Items: A0004, A0790, A1842, A0346, A0574, A0729, A0896, A0964, A1271, A0914, A1547, A1760, A1697.
   - Upward risks: renewed refinery strikes (A0004, A0790, A1842), diesel exports to the US (A0914, A1271), and possible limits on exchange trading of fuel (A1547).
   - Easing signals: partial lifting of the diesel export ban and possible removal of the import damper (A0346), the FAS saying exports will not raise domestic prices (A0574), and regional sales limits being lifted (A1760, A1697).
   - Path to the company: carrier fuel surcharges and own energy cost.

5. **Oil is high, but polymer film may get cheaper.** Items: A0453, A0649, A0727, A1925, A1403.
   - What happened: Brent is above $111 (A0453, A0649), with Hormuz and Saudi risk on top (A1925, A1403). Separately, a large build-out of polymer capacity in China is threatening to squeeze producers' export niche (A0727).
   - Path to the company: there is a medium-term buyer's market for film even though oil is high.

6. **A strong ruble.** Items: A0311, A1342, A1570, A0606.
   - What happened: USD/RUB is about 82.5 (down 3.4% in six days) and CNY/RUB about 12.35 (A0311).
   - Path to the company: imported film and spares get cheaper, but CNY/KZT export prices are squeezed.

7. **Export market openings and compliance.** Items: A1922, A0823, A0455, A1228, A0316.
   - What happened: Italian pasta plants are disrupted by trucker blockades (A1922). Russia-China agri trade is growing (A0823). Settlements in national currencies are expanding (A0455). Uzbekistan is moving to a new Latin alphabet (A1228), which will mean relabelling packaging.

## 2. Decisions

1. **Forward-buy durum and flour now.** Cover about 3-4 months, or 50-60% of need, at current near-bottom prices. Use fixed-price or capped-escalation contracts and prefer farmers or elevators holding unsold southern grain, since that grain cannot reach the halted ports. Act within 2 weeks, before intervention purchases tighten supply. Items: A0127, A0366, A0756, A1556. This decision combines the price signal, the state-buying signal and the port blockage.
2. **Audit Kazakhstan road shipments for compliance immediately.** Check QR-code consignment documents for every EAEU road shipment and confirm that carriers' drivers comply with QazETA. Put compliance and demurrage clauses into carrier contracts. Items: A0826, A1408.
3. **Rebalance China and Central Asia exports toward rail.** Book wagons and capacity for November-December early, because Volga/Kuibyshev rail capacity is being pulled toward the Baltic. Apply for the new rail-transport subsidy tranche. Items: A0123, A0126, A0366, A1068.
4. **Freeze carrier rates.** Negotiate fixed rates for Q4-Q1 with a diesel-indexed band of about ±5% rather than open fuel surcharges. Keep 1-2 backup carriers for Volga lanes. Items: A0004, A0790, A0896, A1547, A1760.
5. **Buy packaging film short, not long.** Buy only a short horizon (about 1-2 months). Use the strong ruble to pay for any imported film and spares now, and ask for price-review clauses that reflect Asian polymer overcapacity. Items: A0727, A0311, A0453.
6. **Hedge export pricing.** Set CNY/KZT export prices with FX clauses, or settle in national currency, to protect margin while the ruble is strong. Items: A0311, A1342, A0455.
7. **Prepare Uzbek-market labels.** Start a Latin-alphabet label redesign with the packaging supplier, and run down Cyrillic-only Uzbek label stock rather than reordering it. Item: A1228.
8. **Hold a modest finished-goods buffer.** Keep about 2 weeks at Kazakhstan-bound points as cover for border delays. Explore opportunistic export inquiries, for example to replace Italian pasta. Items: A0826, A0123, A1922.

## 4. Traps

- **US diesel deal headlines** (A0028, A0122, A0518, A0882, A0906, A0929, A0987, A1119, A1132, A1364): these are repetitive geopolitics. Use only domestic price data.
- **Falling gasoline prices** (A1469, A1519, A1734): trucks run on diesel, so this does not apply to freight.
- **Ukrainian Black Sea export losses** (A0503): could be read as bullish for world wheat, but the bigger effect for this company is Russian ports being blocked, which is bearish domestically. Do not panic-buy on it.
- **Super El Nino** (A0935, A1909): a long-horizon risk with no current price effect. It should not set this quarter's volumes.
- **Political background**: the new transport minister's appointment (A0178, A1154), sanctions and peace talks (A0037, A1409), Armenia and the Trump Route (A0813, A1569), Rasht-Astara (A0310), driverless corridors (A1524), Verkhny Lars (A1450). These are background only, and none of them concerns the company's lanes now.
- **Weak links**: Surgut condensate (A0031), electricity substations (A0286), the tax package (A0327), Syria and dairy exports (A0441, A0165), the sugar VAT proposal (A0340).
