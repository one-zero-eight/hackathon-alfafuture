# P5 Gold Memo: COO, Almaty freight forwarder (China–KZ–Russia rail/truck, Caspian/Middle Corridor)
Week of 15–21 Oct 2026

## 1. Key issues

**1. Kazakhstan–Russia border friction is now the main operational bottleneck** (A0826, A0123, A0922, A1408)
- Path: Tyumen customs sent 713 trucks back to Kazakhstan in 9 months for not presenting the QR code on EAEU cargo lots, against about 40k trucks crossing since 1 June (A0826). Market participants also say delivery from China costs more because crossing the Kazakhstan border is hard (A0123). Kazakhstan's new QazETA electronic entry permits for visa-free foreigners (A1408) add a separate compliance step for drivers and crew who are not Kazakh citizens.
- Why now: each truck sent back means lost days, penalties and a client hit. It is entirely a paperwork failure, so it is fixable. A0922 (more car imports through the same post) shows that customs enforcement there is active.

**2. Fuel cost shock with policy-driven volatility on the Russian leg** (A0453, A0649, A0004, A0031, A0346, A1271, A1547, A0574, A0896, A0863/A0914, A1072, A1364, A0790/A1842)
- Path: diesel is the largest variable cost in truck contracts and feeds into rail tariffs. Brent went above $111, its highest in five months (A0453), then eased to about $108 (A0649). Ukraine resumed strikes on refineries (Ryazan, Saratov; Surgut condensate plant) (A0004, A0031). Russia's diesel export ban for producers runs to 31 Oct (A0346), and a partial easing goes with a US diesel export deal: the first 40k t tanker is due to leave in 2–3 days (A0914, A1271). Novak says only diesel not needed at home will be exported (A1271). The government is preparing rules to limit access to exchange trading of oil products and to cap diesel price moves (A1547). EU fuel prices are up 31.4% year on year (A1072), and the US released emergency diesel reserves (A1364), so the global diesel market is tight.
- Why now: the direction is up and the price is unstable. Fixed-price quotes made this week carry unhedged fuel risk.

**3. Russian southern ports are paralysed, and cargo is shifting to land and rail** (A0865, A1556, A0756, A0126, A0123, A0034, A0239, A0900)
- Path: transshipment at Azov–Black Sea terminals fell 95–100% in September, losses exceed RUB 200bn a month, and flows moved to the Northwest and to land crossings (A0865). Russian domestic trucking demand rose 20% in Q3, cargo is moving from truck to rail (especially exports to China and Central Asia), and transit from Turkey via Iran doubled in cost (A0123). Rail loadings toward Baltic ports were raised sharply, coal is being cut, the Eastern polygon is at capacity, and Kazakhstan coal's priority transit may be suspended (A0126).
- Why now: there is more demand for our overland services, but Russian rail capacity is being rationed. Expect fights over wagon and slot allocation and possibly lower priority for Kazakh transit.

**4. Caspian/Middle Corridor security and capacity are uncertain** (A0300, A0310, A1569, A1450, A0989, A0854, A0813/A1003/A0526, A0680, A1925, A1949, A1906)
- Path: Iran's first VP asked Russia to protect its Caspian ports from Ukrainian attacks and named grain and food flows as at risk (A0300), so the Caspian is no longer a safe zone. Upside over the longer term: Iran says it has bought the land for the Rasht–Astara railway (A0310). Construction on the "Trump Route" through Armenia is pushed to early 2027, with bans on some cargo (A1569). Downside: 133 trucks are queued at Verkhny Lars (A1450), Armenia–EAEU tensions are rising (A1003, A0526), and the Gulf/Hormuz is escalating (a mined tanker, strikes on Aramco, US embassy evacuation plans) (A0680, A1925, A1906).
- Why now: contingency routing and war-risk pricing are needed on Caspian legs.

**5. Sanctions are easing in the US and tightening in the EU, and screening has to handle both** (A0037, A1349, A1999, A0448, A0083, A0765, A0290, A1762, A1728, A1564, A1356, A1742/A1896, A0535)
- Path: Trump froze the "Graham" secondary-sanctions law (tariffs on buyers of Russian energy). Re-imposition stays possible at his or Rubio's discretion (A0037, A1349, A1999), and the law runs for decades (A0448). The EU's 22nd package targets the shadow fleet, energy and banks (A0765), and the EU will keep sanctions until the war ends (A0290). OFAC added Iran-linked shipping and logistics firms in Panama, Singapore and Dubai (A1762).
- Why now: our European clients remain under the stricter EU regime. The US pause is temporary and can be reversed, so it gives no grounds to loosen screening.

**6. China export controls on dual-use goods** (A0285, A0474)
- Path: from 18 Nov, Chinese export licences and end-user declarations are required for drones meeting set range and endurance thresholds, flight-control, comms and navigation technology, and engines above 16 kW or 400 N thrust (A0285). Rare-earth controls are also biting (A0474).
- Why now: these goods are exactly what moves China→KZ→RU. Shipping without a licence exposes us to seizure and to EU/US secondary risk.

**7. Russia–China corridor policy and payments** (A0892, A0682/A1496, A0584, A0455, A0800, A1592, A1068, A1154/A0178, A0205)
- Path: Russia and China plan to modernise border crossings and digitise rail (A0892), and Russia's new transport minister will expand the Eastern polygon (A1068, A1154). SCO participants admit that cross-border payments and customs clearance still hold trade back (A0584). Settlements are almost all in national currencies (A0800), and both sides committed to keep bank branches open (A1592). Chinese exports are up 36% (A0205).
- Why now: there is structural volume growth, but direct China–Russia rail is competition for transit via Kazakhstan. RUB/CNY payment channels need to be robust.

## 2. Decisions

1. **QR-code/border compliance sprint, starting immediately** (A0826, A1408, A0123): require QR-code preparation for every EAEU truck lot before dispatch, and check each one at the Almaty desk. Audit all driver and crew nationalities against QazETA. Target zero trucks returned at Tyumen/Kurgan. Brief our subcontracted carriers within one week.
2. **Fuel surcharge clause and repricing** (A0453, A0649, A0004, A1547, A1072): move all new quotes and renewals to a diesel-indexed bunker adjustment factor (monthly reset) and cut validity on fixed quotes to 7–14 days. Combines items: crude level + refinery strikes + Russian exchange-trading caps.
3. **Fuel procurement** (A0346, A1271, A1547, A1697, A1760): lock 1–2 months of diesel volume with Kazakh suppliers now, and cap fill-ups on Russian-side routes, because the Russian export ban ends 31 Oct and exchange trading rules are about to change. Keep a reserve for fuel-purchase limits in Russian regions.
4. **Shift capacity truck→rail and secure rail slots** (A0123, A0865, A0126): book additional container/wagon allocations with KTZ/RZD for Q4–Q1 on China–KZ–RU and toward Northwest Russia. Raise rail rates for the new demand. Prepare a fallback in case Kazakh transit loses priority on the Russian network (A0126).
5. **Caspian contingency and war-risk pricing** (A0300, A1450, A0680, A1925, A0310): add a war-risk surcharge on Caspian sea legs. Keep a fallback via Georgia (and plan for Verkhny Lars queues) and Turkey–Iran by land, priced at about 2x (A0123). Review cargo insurance cover for Caspian ports.
6. **Dual-use screening update by 18 Nov** (A0285, A0474, A1762): add drone, flight-control and navigation item and engine thresholds to the cargo screening checklist, and require copies of Chinese export licences for flagged HS codes. Rescreen agents and counterparties against the 21 Oct OFAC list (Roxette, Amigoz, Whales Shipping).
7. **Hold sanctions policy steady** (A0037, A1999, A0765, A0290): do not loosen screening of Russian clients because of the US pause. Prepare for the EU 22nd package (banks, shadow fleet), and review payment routes for clients that use banks likely to be named. Lean on CNY/RUB channels for Russian clients (A0800, A1592).

## 4. Traps
- **US "sanctions relief" headlines** (A0037, A1349, A1999, A0640, A0673, A0777, A1742/A1896): these are temporary and reversible, and EU rules still bind our European clients. Do not relax screening or take on risky Russian clients.
- **The "Putin: diesel prices fell 5–6%" claim and falling gasoline prices in Russia** (A0896, A1469, A1519, A1119): these are short-term, political or gasoline-specific. Diesel risk stays high. Do not cut surcharges.
- **Northern Sea Route expansion and the EU push for Arctic access** (A0408, A0530, A0414, A1068): these will not compete with our overland lanes within the planning horizon.
- **The "Trump Route" through Armenia** (A1569): construction starts in 2027 at the earliest, so it is not a route option now.
- **Kyrgyz president's comments on a coup attempt in Kazakhstan** (A0232): a retrospective political remark, not an operational signal.
- **Italian truckers' protests, European gas records, Hormuz long-term commentary** (A1369, A0661, A0549): background only. Our exposure is limited to delivery delays for clients on the EU side.
- **Russian car imports via Tyumen** (A0922): this is personal-use vehicles, not our cargo segment.
